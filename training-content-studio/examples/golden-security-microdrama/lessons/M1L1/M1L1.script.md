## s02_request | setup | O1
template: cartoon
visual: office, Sam (rookie, Finance) at the desk; worried when the email lands; camera pushes in.

rookie: ^ Pay a supplier today, and keep it quiet? {instr: "nervous, eager to please, comic"}
claim: Scenario: Sam in Finance receives an urgent, secret payment request that appears to come from the CEO (fictional example). [general]

## s03_email | red flags | O1
template: email_mock
visual: The fake CEO email with four numbered red-flag call-outs, revealed in narration order.
narrator: Spot the signs. ^ It's not Hana's usual address. ^ It says pay today. ^ The bank details are new. ^ And it asks for secrecy.
claim: Warning sign: a sender address or reply-to address that does not match the usual one. [S01#c1]
claim: Warning sign: urgency or pressure to pay today. [S01#c1]
claim: Warning sign: new or changed bank details. [S01#c1]
claim: Warning sign: a request to keep the payment secret or to bypass the normal process. [S01#c1]
claim: Scenario: the fake email asks for a USD 48,500 payment to a fictional supplier (fictional example). [general]

## s05_decide | decision | O2
visual: Three choices; the video highlights the callback.
narrator: What should Sam do? {pause: 0.5}
narrator: Pay now, reply to the email, ^ or call Hana back on her directory number?
choice: Pay now. It's the CEO. -> s06_paid
choice: Reply to the email to check -> s07_reply
choice: Call Hana on her directory number -> s08_callback (correct)
claim: A request from an executive is verified by calling the executive back on their directory number. [S01#c1]

## s06_paid | consequence | O3
template: statement
visual: Alert statement; mentor voice.
mentor: ^ That money went to the attacker. If money has already been sent, report it to I T Security and Finance immediately.
claim: If you think money has already been sent, report it to IT Security and Finance immediately. [S01#c1]

## s07_reply | consequence | O2
template: statement
visual: Alert statement; mentor voice.
mentor: ^ Your reply went straight to the attacker. Never use a contact detail given in the request itself.
claim: Never use a phone number, link or contact detail given in the request itself. [S01#c1]

## s08_callback | resolution | O2, O3
template: cartoon
visual: office two-shot on the phone: Sam at the desk (left), the real Hana (right); Sam shocked, then relieved.
narrator: Sam calls Hana back on her number in the company directory.
exec: ^ I never sent that. Don't pay it! {instr: "composed but firm"}
rookie: ^ Reporting it to I T Security now. {instr: "relieved, determined"}
claim: Before acting on a payment or bank-detail request, call the requester back on a number already on file in the company directory. [S01#c1]
claim: Report every suspicious payment request to IT Security straight away. [S01#c1]

## s09_rule | rule | O2, O3
template: steps
visual: Three steps: call back, two-person approval, report.
mentor: The rule: ^ call back a number you already have. ^ Bank changes, and payments of ten thousand dollars or more, need two approvers. ^ And report it to I T Security.
claim: Before acting on a request to make a payment or change bank details, call the requester back on a phone number already on file. [S01#c1]
claim: Every payment of USD 10,000 or more needs approval from two authorised people before it is released. [S01#c1]
claim: Every change to bank details needs a second person to approve it after the callback, whatever the amount. [S01#c1]
claim: Report suspicious payment requests to IT Security with the Report Phishing button in Outlook or the IT Security hotline on extension 4400. [S01#c1]

## s10_quiz | check | O2
