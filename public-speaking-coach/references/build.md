# Building a talk in the speaker's voice (presentation + what to say)

Work with the speaker, not for them: ask, propose 2–3 options, let them pick. Use their profile's moves
(§3), skeleton (§5) and guardrails (§8). Write in their language and register (Tamer: Egyptian colloquial,
English for concepts). Never write a full word-for-word script except the opening, the transitions and
the closing; everything else is cues.

## 1. Brief (ask before anything else)
Audience (who, how many, technical level, language), event and slot length, room (stage size, screen,
mic type, confidence monitor?), the ONE action or belief the audience should leave with, what the
organizer expects, and what the speaker wants to be known for after the talk.

## 2. The one sentence
The whole talk in one sentence the speaker would actually say on stage. If it doesn't fit in one breath,
the talk isn't clear yet. Example shape (Tamer): «الـ IT مش Support Function، ده أكبر Leverage Point في
الشركة بعد الـ AI».

## 3. Architecture
- Points: 3 at most for ≤ 20 min, each with a story or example (the speaker's own from the corpus first).
- Use the speaker's natural skeleton per point (Tamer: belief/then → "but now" → own story or scene with
  voiced characters → not X, it's Y → what it means for you).
- Time budget: minutes × the stage pace (profile articulation wpm − 10–15 %, ~70 % of the time is speech)
  = words available. E.g. 20 min × 130 wpm × 0.7 ≈ 1,800 words. Opening ≤ 10 %, closing ≤ 10 %.
- Q&A planned: 3 likely hard questions with one-line answers.

## 4. Opening (first 30 seconds), give 3 options in the speaker's moves
Examples of moves that fit Tamer's profile: the audience's objection asked aloud («طب ليه 80 % من
مشاريع الـ AI بتفشل؟»), a personal story with a year and a number (2001, the monthly report), a scene with
a voiced character («تعال يا عم صلّح الـ Windows»), then-and-now. Not: a definition, an agenda slide, a
bio, a statistic with no story.

## 5. Stories bank
Pull real stories from the speaker's corpus and profile (with source timestamps) before inventing any.
For each: the setup in one line, the turn, the line people will quote, how long it takes (≤ 90 s).
To shape or rewrite a story (five beats, dialogue, the turn, exact details, the Button), use the
storytelling-techniques skill when it's installed next to this one.

## 6. Slides
One idea per slide; title = a claim, not a topic; numbers big; little text (≤ 6 words where possible);
a black slide for each story; the speaker's notes are cues: the claim, the story keyword, the transition
line, the time mark. Arabic RTL: never start a line with an English word; English terms in their own
spans.

## 7. Marked script (for rehearsal)
For opening, transitions and closing: mark `/` short pause, `//` long pause (1–2 s), **bold** the one
stressed word per sentence, `[→ left]` eye zone or move, `[character]` voice change. The rest: cue
bullets per slide.

## 8. Closing
Callback to the opening story or question + the one sentence + the action. Last line memorized; no "that's
it, thank you" as the last words (thanks before the last line, or after a pause).

## 9. Rehearsal plan
Rehearsal 1 out loud with slides (structure and timing) → fix structure. Rehearsal 2 recorded standing
(see SKILL.md) → review → max 3 fixes. Rehearsal 3 recorded → compare with 2. A final run the day
before: opening + closing only, plus one full run if time allows.
