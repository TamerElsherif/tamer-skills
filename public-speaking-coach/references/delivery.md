# Delivery by setting: stage (default), camera, online

The speaker's profile (speaking-style `profile.md`) says how they naturally talk. This file says how each
setting changes the delivery **relative to that baseline**. Never give the absolute "rules" of generic
speaking books: shift the speaker's own numbers, keep their moves.

## Stage (live audience, the default)

The room is bigger than a podcast table: everything the speaker does must reach the back row, and there is
no editing, no close-up, no second take.

| | Podcast / conversation | Stage | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pace | the profile's articulation wpm | **10–15 % slower** | The room needs time; Arabic + English terms need it more |
| Pauses | ~0.4 s, few long ones | **1–2 s at every transition, ~1 s before every punchline**, 3 s after a big question | Silence on stage reads as confidence, not a gap |
| Volume | mic does the work | **project to the back row**, then drop for intimate moments | The contrast is the energy |
| Pitch movement | profile value | **+30–50 %** (the inside-a-turn semitones) | Flat delivery dies faster on stage than on camera |
| Gestures | small, below the table | **bigger, at chest height or above, away from the body**; hold the gesture to the end of the phrase | Seen from 20 m away |
| Eyes | the co-host | **zones**: left / centre / right, one person per thought (3–5 s), front and back rows | Every section of the room feels spoken to |
| Body | seated | **planted feet on key points, move only on transitions** (walk = new idea); no pacing, no swaying | Movement with meaning; random movement steals attention |
| Hands with nothing to do | fidgeting, ring, fingers | relaxed at the sides or loosely together at waist height; clicker in one hand only | Fidgeting reads as nerves from the back |
| Fillers | natural | trim the openers of sentences; replace restarts with a pause | Fillers grow under stress |
| Stories / characters | told | **performed**: change voice and posture for each character, look at an imaginary person | Acting carries the room |

**Stage structure facts**
- The first 30 s decide attention: no "thank you for having me" warm-up as the opening line, no apology,
  no "let me introduce myself" before the hook. Thanks can come after the hook.
- People remember the start, the end and the stories. Put the core message in all three.
- Signpost more than in conversation: a live audience can't rewind. One roadmap line at the start
  («هقولكم حاجتين…») and a one-line bridge between sections.
- Slides: one idea per slide, the speaker looks at the audience, not the screen (glance at the laptop / the
  confidence monitor). Never read a slide aloud. Black slide (B key) for the key story.
- Microphones: handheld = 2–3 fingers from the chin, keep it there when turning the head; lavalier = don't
  touch the chest, careful with shirt and jewellery noise. Check the mic and clicker before the talk.
- Q&A: repeat or paraphrase the question for the room, answer to the whole room, not only the asker; a
  bridging line for questions outside scope; end Q&A with your own closing line, not the last question.
- Time: the talk is 10 % shorter than the slot (start late, applause, clicker trouble).

**Nerves (practical, no theory)**
- Breathe out longer than in (4 in, 6–8 out) for 2 minutes before going up.
- Memorize the first 3 sentences and the closing word for word; everything else stays as cues.
- Arrive early, stand on the stage, look at the back row, say the first line out loud once.
- A blank: pause, look at the cue card or slide, say "the point is…" and the slide's message. The audience
  doesn't know the script.

## Camera (recorded talk, video, reel)

- Eyes **on the lens** for the key lines (the lens is the audience); looking away to think is fine mid-thought.
- Energy +20 % over conversation: the camera flattens it. Smaller gestures than stage, **inside the frame**
  (chest height, not below the table or the mic arm).
- Framing: vertical 9:16 crops the sides: gestures close to the body; face 3/4 toward the lens, not profile.
- Pace can stay close to the profile's natural pace; edits remove long pauses, so keep pauses deliberate.
- First 3 seconds = hook (reels): eyes up, voice up, no warm-up.

## Online lecture (Zoom / Teams / webinar)

- Camera at eye level, look at the lens when making a point, at the screen when reading chat.
- Energy +30 %: small window, no room energy to feed on. Smile more than on stage.
- Interaction every 5–7 minutes: a question to the chat, a poll, a name. Silence online feels longer: shorter
  pauses than on stage (0.5–1 s), and say what you are doing («ثانية أفتح الشريحة…»).
- Visual change every 1–2 minutes (slide, annotation, demo) to hold attention.
- Audio quality matters more than video: a headset or close mic, quiet room.
